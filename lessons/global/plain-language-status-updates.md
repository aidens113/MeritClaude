# Report status in plain English

On 2026-10-07, after a status update built from internal labels (lane letters with cause codes such as D4-1, run ids, commit hashes and file paths), Aiden replied only "plain englihs". He asks often where things stand, and he needs to understand the answer at a glance.

When reporting status or progress, lead with plain sentences: what was tried, what happened, what it means for the goal, and what happens next. Name things by what they are ("the cart task", "the check that judges the result"), not by internal identifiers. Keep run ids, cause codes, commit hashes and file names out of the main answer; add them only when he asks for "full details", or when he needs one to act. Short tables are fine when each cell is plain words. This complements answering direct questions first and reporting progress as it happens: the update still comes promptly, just in words he can read without the working document.

Why it matters: internal codes are the agents' shorthand, not his. A report he has to decode costs him the time the update was meant to save, and makes it harder for him to judge whether the work is on track.
